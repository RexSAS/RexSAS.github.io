I started at eleven with HTML and CSS, building pages for the fun of seeing them load. Since then I have moved down the stack: C and C++ at school, Java for Minecraft server plugins, Python for a hand-tracking experiment, and Dart with Flutter for a notes app I still use every day.

In 2023 the CyberChallenge.IT programme turned curiosity into method. I learned to read network traffic, take binaries apart and attack web applications, and then to write the patch that stops the attack. A year later I went back to teach it.

From the lab in Ferrara to the final in Turin

CyberChallenge.IT is Italy’s training programme for young security talent, run by the CINI Cybersecurity National Lab.

At the University of Ferrara I followed its 75 hours of training, placed fourth of 20 in the local round and joined 0xFE, the six-person team for the national attack/defence final in Turin. After eight hours against 42 teams from across Italy, we finished 22nd. The next year the university asked me back as a tutor.
