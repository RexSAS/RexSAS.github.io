CyberChallenge 2023

Four months of training at the University of Ferrara, eight hours of attack and defence in Turin, and a return the following year from the other side of the desk.

The programme

From January to July 2023 I took part in the CyberChallenge.IT training programme at the Department of Mathematics and Computer Science, University of Ferrara.

The programme is run by the Cybersecurity National Lab of CINI, the Italian inter-university consortium for computer science. It is 75 hours of lectures and labs, warmed up by smaller events like the Cyber Cup series, then a local competition: eight hours, individual, on 31 May 2023. I finished fourth of 20, and the top six became 0xFE, the Ferrara team for the national final. Four of the six of us were still in high school.

What I learned

Cryptography
Symmetric and asymmetric key ciphers, and the techniques for decrypting them when they are used badly.
Network security
Analysing network traffic packet by packet, and building isolated environments with Docker.
Web security
JavaScript fundamentals and the attacks of the web: SQL injection, path traversal, XSS, CSRF, code injection and command injection.
Access control
Working confidently in Linux, understanding how users, permissions and the system are put together.
Software security
The structure of executable files, reverse engineering, and debugging binaries you have no source for.
Hardware security
A first look at physical attack techniques against devices.

The final: attack and defence

On 29 June 2023, at the International Training Centre of the ILO in Turin, 42 teams played eight hours of attack/defence. Every team gets the same vulnerable services and must keep them running while attacking everyone else’s. A round goes like this:

  1. Find the flawRead the service, source or binary, until the vulnerability shows itself.
  2. Write the patchClose it in your own service without breaking what it does.
  3. Build the exploitUse the same flaw against the teams that have not closed it yet.

See the full national ranking

0xFE finished 22nd of 42 with over 26,500 points. Across the whole final: about 234 competitors, more than 410,000 flags stolen and 800 GB of network traffic.

Tools in hand

Languages: mainly Python, with working knowledge of Assembly, JavaScript, SQL, PHP, C and C++.

A year later, as a tutor

Because of my ranking in Ferrara, I returned to the programme in 2024 on the other side: as a tutor.

I supported students in the university labs, teaching what I knew best from the final: network security, software security and attack/defence play. I helped them prepare for the national tournament, so that this year’s team could go further than we did.

The University of Ferrara wrote about it: the UniFe team for CyberChallenge 2023, the CyberChallenge 2023 final.
